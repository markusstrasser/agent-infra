**Verdict:** Across four research lanes and nine epochs, 71 cases were grounded on primary documents, organized by mechanism rather than by field or political side, with 65 anchors re-verified by the parent against the primaries and captured in a re-runnable probe. The construction-level checklist the research skill already carries (items 1–33: ledger, window, base, gross/net, model-vs-measurement) explains fewer than half the cases; the rest are a **process layer** that the checklist did not have: who controls the instrument, the definition, the series, the extract date, the consensus, the retraction, the data access, and the report. That layer is where "science" and "economics" were used politically, and it is practiced in both directions with the same structure: signature letters with no magnitude (126 vs 230→369 economists on one bill), harm criteria at journals and terminology orders at agencies, a union's sentences and a trade association's standards both entering federal text (one diffable and unattributed, the other attributed and undiffable), trust series whose partisan sign flips with who holds the White House, and two true percentages of the same 103 studies quoted against each other in the Cass Review dispute (98% not rated high quality, 60% synthesised). The three largest measured effects are instrument effects on fixed data: US maternal mortality **+144% under the death-certificate checkbox rule vs flat (10.2→10.4 per 100,000) on the same records**; mass-shooting counts differing **31.5×** between databases for one year and **4.8×** after harmonizing definitions; and the same public-events ban credited with **71% of the R_t reduction in Sweden and under 2% elsewhere** because it happened to be the last intervention in the model's parameterization. The finding that matters for the harness is about the instrument used to build this library: **roughly a third of the parent's own training-memory leads were wrong or overshot in their strong form, in both political directions**, and every one was caught only by reading the primary. On a politically charged corpus, the model's recall is a lead generator and nothing more. [SOURCE: lane files and probe cited below; INFERENCE where labelled]

# Politicized statistics, 2016–2026: the mechanism library

## 0. Question, method, instrument caveat

Markus asked (2026-09-16) whether `/analyze` should absorb "more ways stats lie" and how, over the last ten years, "science", "stats" and "economics" were used for political purposes. The answer to the first question was already partly on disk: the research skill's `quant-bias-checklist.md` (33 items, construction level) is the `/analyze` adjunct, and the immigration-research crime-statistics memo of the same morning had just grounded twelve mechanisms at grade A by walking one statistic's production pipeline stage by stage. The gap was the process layer above the author: institutions. This memo is the evidence for that layer.

**Method.** Four lanes, each dispatched to an Opus researcher with 12-turn epochs and a write-first checkpoint file: A instrument and agency · B consensus, authority, suppression · C construction for policy and litigation · D science as rhetoric and institution. Lanes A, C and D ran two epochs; B ran three. Every lead the parent supplied was labelled training-memory and had to end GROUNDED (primary document, verbatim quote, URL, date read, grade A–D), CONTRADICTED (the source says otherwise; kept), or NOT FOUND (queries logged). Selection rule: a number, model or consensus claim **used** for a political purpose (policy, hearing, court, mandate, campaign, editorial) between 2016-01 and 2026-09; replication failures without political use belong in `adversarial-case-library.md`, not here.

**Both-directions rule.** For every mechanism the lanes had to seek a case from each political direction or write "no counter-direction case found" with the queries run. This is the mitigation for a documented property of the instrument: the model's dispositions on politically charged topics are asymmetric (immigration-research `notes/llm-bias-caveat.md`). Section 4 reports how the rule performed against the parent's own leads.

**Verification.** Sixty-five anchors, each one or more verbatim numbers or phrases, were re-fetched by the parent from the primary documents (PubMed and EuropePMC records, Crossref abstracts, court and agency PDFs, Federal Register raw text, Wayback `id_` captures for hosts that block scripted fetches) and are encoded in `research/scratch/2026-09-16-politicized-stats/verify_anchors.py` (65/65 at 2026-09-16 15:08 CEST). Re-run it before citing any anchor externally; it exits non-zero on drift. Route notes for hosts that refuse curl are inside the lane files.

**What this memo is not.** It does not adjudicate which side of any dispute was right. Where two constructions exist, the finding is the range and who chose which endpoint. Where a lead was wrong, the lane says so and the memo keeps it.

## 2. What the layer looks like

The construction items (1–33) describe how an author builds a misleading number. The process layer describes who controls the number's existence: the checkbox on a death certificate (34), the decision to stop publishing (35), the date the extract was pulled (36), the name given to the residual (37), the tenure of the statistician (38), the cut the producer holds back (39), the letter that stands in for a measurement (40, 47), the scenario that becomes the forecast (41), the reason printed on a retraction (42), the number said for its effect on the audience (43), the grade that is read one way for a friend and another for an enemy (44), the dataset nobody outside can see (45), the definition widened for the headline (46), the criterion a journal or an executive applies to already-published work (48), the sentence an outside party wrote into the file (49), the dashboard whose caveats did not survive its use (50), the report whose conclusion was inferable from its roster (51), and the trust series that measures who holds power (52). The two most transferable checks found this round are questions, not accusations: *read the denominator definition, not the category label* (CDC excluded the partially vaccinated from both sides of its fraction; Utah folded them into "Unvaccinated" and published a rate ratio), and *which of two co-existing instruments is this number from?* (the White House maternal-health blueprint charts PMSS pregnancy-related deaths, not the NVSS series the checkbox critique refutes; Colorado published surveillance deaths and certificate deaths side by side and said so before either side quoted one). A third check came from the last epoch: *which predicate is this percentage of?* In the Cass Review dispute, "98% of the evidence was ignored" and "nearly 60% was synthesised" rest on the same 103 studies, of which 101 were not rated high quality and 60 entered the synthesis; the misuse was the verb that turned "not rated high quality" into "ignored".

## 3. Checklist section §I — the items this library grounds

The wording below is the draft that went to cross-model review on 2026-09-16. The reviewed version is canonical in the research skill's `quant-bias-checklist.md`, section I (skills `e2090a3`). Review changes: 47 merged into 40; 46 split into 46 (litigation-grade statistics) and 47 (definitional expansion); the Flaxman/Soltesz credit-attribution anchor moved to construction item 14; 37 narrowed to residual categories; the universal claims in 35 and 38 were removed; every item now ends in an action. Anchors are the grade-A/B cases above; items without such a case were dropped.

34. **Instrument change read as trend.** Before reading any trend, list the instrument changes in the window and show the series under both rules; quote the definition, not the accusation built on it (the 14-day rule is real; "counted the vaccinated as unvaccinated" is true of Utah's product and false of CDC's); never cite a change the producer refused to certify.
35. **Series discontinuation or removal.** An absent or restarted series is a finding about the institution: date it, name who decided, ask for the back-cast; "retired rather than fixed" is its own tell. No case was found of a government suspending a series against its own interest.
36. **Extract date is a variable.** Carry the extract date with every administrative number; compare like vintage to like vintage; a revision is the instrument's documented behavior until shown otherwise, and a revision that runs against the producer's interest is evidence against capture.
37. **Residual category renamed as a group.** Find the residual, who renamed it, and the share that is measurement failure; read the denominator definition, not the category label.
38. **Statistical-agency interference.** Agency independence is part of provenance; test a manipulation claim against the releases produced after the intervention; the attack on an unwelcome number targets the producer's status and never publishes a competing construction — in both directions (BLS, ELSTAT).
39. **Selective release by the producer.** A stated fear of misinterpretation discloses a directional filter; ask what the producer holds and did not publish.
40. **Consensus manufacturing.** A consensus claim is a sociological datum: quote the denominator (97.1% of 32.6%), what the signatories measured (signature lists have none), the private record (Slack vs paper vs sworn testimony), and whether the disclosure was ever corrected (the Lancet added a competing-interests addendum; the claims stand).
41. **Scenario-as-baseline.** A scenario is a bound, and the report usually says so; quote the conditional with the number, and name the parameter that decides which policy gets the credit (Flaxman's last-NPI slot; Report 9's "(unlikely)").
42. **Retraction reason as evidence.** Quote the stated reason verbatim; separate the error-driven correction from the citation-driven escalation; a retraction without a named error is not evidence against the finding, and one with a named error is not evidence of persecution.
43. **Communication instrument vs estimand.** If a stated number is a function of a poll of the audience or of an anticipated panic, it is not the estimand; state the interval the speaker actually holds. Matched cases on both sides at the same grade.
44. **Evidence-hierarchy asymmetry.** One standard, both directions, same paragraph; name the estimand the trials identify (a promotion policy is not a device); do not slide between certainty in an effect (GRADE) and quality of a study (Newcastle-Ottawa); when two sides quote different percentages of one body of evidence, name each predicate (98% "not rated high quality" and 60% "synthesised" are both true of the same 103 studies); check which review a policy actually cites and its date, and whether a promised correction ever landed.
45. **Data-access refusal.** Non-replicable plus interested party equals a brief, not a measurement; grade D until data appear; check the time order of the design; a statutory refusal that survives an adverse judgment (Tiahrt) is the strong form.
46. **Litigation-grade statistics; definitional expansion.** Check the affidavit's population assumption and the independence assumption first; print the operational definition next to the count and the count under the narrow definition; when the producer publishes all the cuts (ADL, GVA), the politicized act is which one gets quoted.
47. **Authority-by-letter.** A letter with credentials and no magnitude, horizon or counterfactual is unfalsifiable by any later series; laureate-count does not discriminate when both sides have one.
48. **Post-publication criteria at journals and agencies.** A harm criterion or a terminology directive applied to already-published work changes who decides, not what is true; note which one had an external check (the court order) and which had none.
49. **Outside text in the file.** The measurement is whose text is in the document and whether a reader can tell: diff it when it is printed (AFT: 96.7% words, 0 sentences, no trigger) and say so when it cannot be (API standards incorporated by reference).
50. **Instrument whose owner is its only auditor.** A dashboard, a platform's error rate, or a report revised in place by its author: state that the caveats exist and that they did not survive the use; do not upgrade "misused" to "fabricated" without a named fabrication.
51. **Report built to a conclusion.** Author roster, timeline, review status and correction practice are the construction; a roster-inferable report can still contain correct claims, and a fabricated citation is a different defect from a contrarian one.
52. **Trust as an outcome.** A partisan trust series flips sign with control of the executive and cannot identify a politicization event by itself; independents are the control; state the instrument (motives of scientists vs confidence in institutions).

Mechanically checkable candidates: 36 (extract date present near any administrative number) and 34 (an instrument-change line present when a trend is claimed). Do not build the hook until a project shows repeat violations after the checklist.
