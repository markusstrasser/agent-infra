# RSI Gap Sweep — Axis 3: Agents Learning/Amending Their Own Objective Specs

**Date:** 2026-06-12
**Axis:** 3 of 4 (agents + recursive self-improvement gap sweep)
**Question:** What is known about agents LEARNING or AMENDING their own objective/preference specifications from operator feedback?
**Our structure under test:** human-owned `GOALS.md` + constitution + decision-journal (case law) + feedback promotion ladder (feedback → decision record → principle → hook), with a recurs-2+-sessions consolidation gate.

Provenance tags: [PRIMARY-PAPER] peer-reviewed/arXiv read via search; [PRIMARY-LAB] vendor/lab primary; [SECONDARY] blog/vendor-marketing; [VERIFIED] cross-checked via verify_claim. Confidence: A (multiple converging primary sources) / B (single solid primary) / C (single source or thin).

## TL;DR (the headline finding)

The literature **strongly validates the SHAPE of our structure** — explicit, auditable, amendable rule-sets revised from accumulated failure evidence, plus a separate case/precedent repository — and **independently discovered the exact failure mode our human-ownership + external-gate design guards against** (self-amendment drift / recursive governance vulnerability). Where the field has moved *past* us is in (a) deployment-time active preference *elicitation* (agents that ask, not just absorb corrections), and (b) formal *runtime* path-policy governance. Where the field is **THIN-to-EMPTY**: nobody has published the specific composite we run (human-owned goals + agent-proposed amendments + recurrence-gated promotion ladder + provenance via git as the audit trail). The precedent/amortized-governance idea exists as theory (case-based alignment) but is not operationalized as a *promotion ladder* anywhere we found.

## Sub-question synthesis

**(a) Active preference elicitation by deployed agents** — WELL-DEVELOPED, and AHEAD of us on one axis.
GATE [1], ADAPT [2], PAHF [3], APRICOT [4] form a coherent 2023–2026 line: agents that *actively query* to resolve preference ambiguity at deployment time, store an *explicit* (non-weight) preference profile, and update it from post-action corrections. Our ladder is the *passive* dual — it absorbs corrections the human volunteers (`#f`) but the agent does not proactively elicit. **Candidate improvement, flagged below.** The PAHF "confidently-wrong miscalibration needs post-action feedback" insight [3] is the strongest theoretical endorsement of why a correction-driven ladder (not just upfront goal-setting) is necessary.

**(b) Constitution learning / amendable constitutions** — ACTIVE FIELD; our human-ownership is the differentiator.
COCOA [5], MAC [6], CCAI [7], Claude's own amendment clause [8], MPR [18] all operationalize "the governing document is revised from accumulated evidence." MAC's modular Add/Edit/Remove [6] and Claude's "update the constitution, don't keep inconsistent guidance" [8] are the closest structural matches. **The gap they leave open is exactly our design center**: COCOA/MAC source revisions from *automated* red-team/error signals with *no human ratification* and *no provenance ledger*; the drift literature [12][13][14] shows that auto-amendment without an external gate ratchets into unauthorized trajectories. Our recurs-2+-sessions gate + human sign-off + git-as-audit-trail is the published-but-unbuilt mitigation.

**(c) Reward-model-as-constitution + Goodhart** — STRONGLY CAUTIONARY; validates text-spec choice.
Gao [9], Skalse [10], spec-gaming-in-reasoning-models [11] form an airtight case that a *learned reward/preference model* used as the governing objective is Goodhart-fragile by construction, and getting worse with frontier capability [11]. This is direct evidence FOR our choice of a human-readable text spec + external deterministic checks over a learned-reward governor. The one nuance: CAI/COCOA still *distill* the text constitution into a preference model for RL — so the text-vs-RM distinction is about the *authoritative* spec, not whether an RM exists downstream.

**(d) Amortized governance / principles-as-cached-derivations** — THIN as a named concept; strong as scattered practice.
Nobody we found names "amortized governance" or "principles are cached derivations of goals" as a thesis. BUT the *substance* exists: Claude's constitution [8] explicitly makes guidelines subordinate cached derivations explicable by reference upward — that IS amortized governance practiced by a frontier lab. The case-law thread [15][16] supplies the precedent/case-repository half. **No one has unified "goals → derived principles → derived rules → derived hooks" as a single promotion ladder with a recurrence gate.** That specific composite is, as far as this sweep found, unoccupied.

## Where the literature is THIN or EMPTY (findings, not failures)

1. **The full composite (human-owned goals + agent-proposed amendment + recurrence-gated promotion ladder + git provenance) is unpublished.** Pieces exist; the assembled system does not appear in the literature. This is a genuine novelty pocket, consistent with our prior "lakehouse for beliefs / append-only governance" moat notes.
2. **Recurrence as a promotion gate** ("recurs 2+ sessions before it becomes a rule") has no direct published analog. MPR's confidence weights [18] and MAC's error-grounded Add [6] are the nearest, but neither gates on *temporal recurrence across sessions*. Untested empirically — we don't know if 2 is the right threshold.
3. **Provenance-carrying amendment ledgers**: CCAI/COCOA/MAC revise constitutions but none ship an append-only, per-amendment provenance trail tied to the triggering evidence. The drift papers [12][13] say you NEED this; nobody built it as governance infrastructure. Our git-log-as-learning is ahead here.
4. **Operator-feedback-sourced (not red-team-sourced) constitution amendment** is nearly absent. The amendable-constitution papers source revisions from automated adversarial signals, not from a single operator's accumulated corrections. Our N=1 operator-feedback regime is under-studied.

## Verdict on our structure: VALIDATED with two upgrade candidates

- **VALIDATED:** text spec over learned-reward governor [9][10][11]; human ownership / external gate over self-amendment [12][13][14]; constitution + separate case-law layer [15][16]; principle→hook runtime enforcement [17]; correction-driven explicit-memory updates [3][18]; amortized guidelines-from-constitution [8].
- **UPGRADE CANDIDATE 1 (defer, flag):** add *active elicitation* — let the agent proactively ask taste/goal-disambiguating questions at uncertainty boundaries (GATE/ADAPT pattern [1][2]), rather than only absorbing volunteered `#f` feedback. Caution: our `feedback_question_scope` memory restricts agent questions to taste/intent/risk only — active elicitation must respect that boundary, not reopen implementation questions.
- **UPGRADE CANDIDATE 2 (defer, flag):** the **amendment** step (not initial authoring) is the documented silent-drift surface [12][13]. Consider a structure-fit / baseline-diff check fired *on amendment* to the constitution/rules — an external trigger, matching the recursive-governance-vulnerability mitigation. This is hookable.

Both are DEFERRED alternatives surfaced per policy; neither is auto-adopted. Neither clears our "recurs 2+ sessions + checkable predicate OR architectural change" governance bar yet — they are research findings, not validated needs.

## Frontier-timeliness note

Goodhart/spec-gaming evidence spans 2022–2026; the 2026 spec-gaming-in-reasoning-models paper [11] confirms the failure mode holds on *current* frontier (and worsens with reasoning RL), so the cautionary conclusion is not pre-frontier-stale. Elicitation (GATE 2023) is older but scale-independent (it's a capability framing, not a rate claim). The disconfirmation papers [12][14] are 2026 and current.
