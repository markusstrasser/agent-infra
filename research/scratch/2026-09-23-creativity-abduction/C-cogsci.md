**Verdict:** Five findings are real gaps with abstract-verified support. For /analyze: (1) the Mamede structured-reflection protocol, gated on case complexity (a generic "slow down" instruction does nothing); (2) switching from hypothesis-space to experiment-space search when hypothesis generation stalls, plus an anomaly ledger. For /brainstorm: (3) problem reformulation before ideation; (4) McCaffrey generic-parts decomposition; (5) recalibrating distance, because field data show that nearer sources correlate with better ideas. The serial-order effect is robust but tiny (≤3.5% of variance), so it does not justify a rule. Incubation, strong inference, BVSR and Geneplore do not transfer as procedures.

## Proposed skill edits (ranked by evidence strength × transfer confidence)

1. **analyze/lenses/hypotheses-ach.md: add the structured reflection protocol with a complexity gate.** Evidence: items 11–13; two meta-analyses, g=0.20, guided reflection the most consistent. For the leading hypothesis and each alternative, fill three columns: *supporting findings*, *contradicting findings*, *findings expected but absent*. Run it when the case is complex or atypical, **or when it resembles a recently seen incident or memory entry** (the availability-bias trigger in Mamede 2010). Add one line saying "be careful / think thoroughly" instructions are measured inert (Norman 2014: 44.5% vs 45.0%), so the columns are the intervention.

2. **analyze/references/reasoning-principles.md §2 (Phase 2): add a switch to experiment space and an anomaly ledger.** Evidence: items 15–18. When no hypothesis dominates after one ACH pass, or enumeration yields only variants, stop generating hypotheses: pick the cheapest probe expected to split the space, run it, and induce from the results (Klahr & Dunbar). Keep an anomaly ledger of surprising observations with the scope of each surprise. Dismissing an anomaly requires naming the discounting move (Chinn & Brewer) and its test. Two or more anomalies with a shared pattern force a reframe instead of local patches.

3. **brainstorm/SKILL.md Step 1: reformulate the problem before generating.** Evidence: item 3; consistent direction across ≥4 studies, sizes unknown. Before Step 2, write 3–5 distinct constructions of the problem (differing goal, the obstacle being attacked, the constraint set) and generate against at least two. Record them in `coverage.json` as a construction axis. The literature says this matters most when the problem is ill-defined or its cues conflict, so `--quick` on a well-specified task can skip it.

4. **brainstorm/SKILL.md Step 3 + references/domain-pools.md: recalibrate analogical distance.** Evidence: item 4, three abstracts plus a field study that contradicts the current wording. Replace "Distant domains, not adjacent ones — the discomfort is the mechanism" with a statement of the tradeoff: far domains raise novelty and quality *variance*, sources that are too far hurt, and nearer sources correlated with better-rated ideas in field data. Each domain-forcing round should include one mid-distance domain that shares the problem's *function or relational structure*, plus the distant ones. Prefer less-common source examples in knowledge injection. Transfer caveat: the human cost of far mapping may be lower for LLMs, so treat this as a variance/quality tradeoff rather than a ban on far domains.

5. **brainstorm/SKILL.md Step 3: new opt-in axis "3f: Generic parts".** Evidence: item 1; one study, 67%, no replication found. Decompose the current design into components and sub-parts. Re-describe each part without any use-implying word (for example "retry queue" becomes "ordered durable list with timestamps"). Generate ideas that use the exposed features. Label it single-study, and make it opt-in like `contradiction`/`blend` until a local A/B shows yield.

6. **brainstorm/references/llmx-dispatch.md: no idea text across workers before synthesis.** Evidence: items 5–7. Perturbation-round payloads carry banned-paradigm *labels*, not example idea text, and parallel workers never see each other's output before Step 4. Idea exchange narrows the domains explored (Kohn & Smith 2010), and examples lower category breadth (Sio et al. 2015). I did not read the current payloads in this run, so check whether they already comply before editing.

7. **analyze/lenses/hypotheses-ach.md, one line.** Evidence: items 14 and 19. Each alternative must be one you would credit at ≥10%, because strawman alternatives do not debias. Do not score simplicity or scope as evidence when base rates are available.

## Rejected / folklore / no transfer

- **Incubation** (Sio & Ormerod): the effect is real for humans, but its mechanisms have no analogue in stateless calls. No transfer.
- **Serial-order effect as a "don't truncate" rule:** real but tiny (≤3.5% of variance, Barbot 2026). Denial cascade already handles the attractor basin, so a new rule would add cost without benefit. Low value.
- **Platt strong inference:** an advocacy essay. Critiques (e.g. Davis 2006, *Perspect Biol Med*; not retrieved here, so SECONDARY) find no evidence that fields using it progress faster. Its useful core, discriminating experiments, is already in ACH and edit 2. FOLKLORE as a productivity claim.
- **Chamberlin multiple working hypotheses:** an 1890 essay with no controlled test. The procedure is already in ACH. Hirt & Markman is the empirical version (item 14).
- **Thagard ECHO / explanatory coherence:** a computational model with no procedural delta beyond ACH plus the evidence matrix.
- **Lipton loveliness vs. likeliness:** philosophy. Its empirical correlate is the simplicity/scope bias (item 19), which calls for a guard, not a promotion.
- **Magnani manipulative abduction:** conceptual. Its operational content is Klahr & Dunbar experiment-space search (edit 2).
- **Geneplore, Simonton BVSR, Mednick remote association:** descriptive theories. BVSR's "blind variation" is already implemented as perturbation plus later selection. The RAT is a measure, not a technique. Ward's "path of least resistance" (people retrieve the most accessible category exemplar) is the attractor basin that denial cascade already targets; I did not retrieve Ward's abstract.
- **Quantity breeds quality** (Osborn): Reinig & Briggs 2008 (no abstract obtained) reportedly find diminishing returns. SECONDARY. The skill already pairs volume with structure, so no edit.
- **Croskerry cognitive forcing / diagnostic time-out / premature closure** (Graber 2005, not retrieved): folded into edit 1. Cognitive forcing had a positive but less consistent effect than guided reflection (Lambe 2016). The premature-closure prevalence claim is SECONDARY.
