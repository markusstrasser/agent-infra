## Item 40 — Consensus manufacturing

### 40-A. Cook et al. 2013 "97% consensus" — the denominator is 32.6% of the corpus — GROUNDED (A)
**Number as used:** "97% of scientists agree" / "Ninety-seven percent of scientists agree: #climate change is real,
man-made and dangerous." — @BarackObama, 16 May 2013, linking to the paper's coverage. Used thereafter in EPA
rulemaking messaging, congressional floor statements and the 2015 Paris run-up.
**Primary record:** Cook et al., *Environmental Research Letters* 8:024024 (2013), doi 10.1088/1748-9326/8/2/024024,
read 2026-09-16. Abstract, verbatim: "**66.4% of abstracts expressed no position on AGW, 32.6% endorsed AGW, 0.7%
rejected AGW and 0.3% were uncertain about the cause of global warming. Among abstracts expressing a position on
AGW, 97.1% endorsed the consensus position that humans are causing global warming.**"
**Construction:** the 97.1% denominator is the 4,014 abstracts that took a position (3,896 endorse + 78 reject +
40 uncertain), not the 11,944 abstracts screened. Two thirds of the corpus is excluded from the ratio before it is
computed. The paper's own author self-rating arm reports: "Among self-rated papers **not** expressing a position on
AGW in the abstract, **53.8%** were self-rated as endorsing the consensus" — i.e. when the no-position abstracts are
polled directly, they do not break 97/3; they break ~54/46 (of self-rated, 35.5% of authors returned ratings).
**Alternative construction:** 3,896 / 11,944 = **32.6%** of screened abstracts explicitly endorsed AGW; the *implicit*
endorsement categories (1–3) dominate the endorsing set. The paper never claims "97% of scientists"; the political
use converts a ratio-among-position-takers over *abstracts* into a headcount over *scientists*.
**Grade:** A (peer-reviewed, open data, the paper states its own denominator plainly).
**Direction:** served climate-policy advocacy (left-coded in US politics).
**Mechanism:** denominator substitution inside a consensus claim; the paper is not the misuse — the paper publishes
the number that falsifies the political rendering, in its own abstract.

### 40-B. Nature's 2020 Biden endorsement — measured backlash — GROUNDED (A)
**The political use:** *Nature* editorial, 14 Oct 2020, endorsing Joe Biden — the journal's stated rationale was that
Trump's record on COVID-19 and science warranted it.
**Primary record:** Floyd Jiuyun Zhang, "Political endorsement by Nature and trust in scientific expertise during
COVID-19," *Nature Human Behaviour* 7, 2023, doi 10.1038/s41562-023-01537-5 (preregistered RCT, n≈4,260 US adults,
summer 2021; replication data at doi 10.7910/dvn/kbirpn). Read 2026-09-16.
**Measured magnitudes (verbatim):** "The endorsement had large negative effects on Trump supporters' trust in *Nature*'s
informedness (t(3,881) = −16.49; P < 0.001; **β = −0.854**; 95% CI (−0.955, −0.752)) and impartiality (t(3,881) = −12.71;
P < 0.001; **β = −0.633**)." "The endorsement led to a statistically significant **14.2-percentage-point reduction** in the
frequency at which Trump supporters requested *Nature* articles … **a 38.3% decline** relative to control Trump supporters,
who requested *Nature* articles 37.1% of the time." Biden-supporter effects: β = +0.108 (informed, P<0.001) and +0.045
(impartial, P = 0.151) — "positive but small." Spillover: trust in US scientists in general, Trump supporters
β = −0.130 (P = 0.015, informed) and −0.161 (P = 0.002, unbiased); Biden supporters +0.049 (P = 0.148) and +0.016
(P = 0.604). Abstract: "I found little evidence that the endorsement changed views about Biden and Trump."
**Alternative construction:** the endorsement's intended quantity (vote shift) is ~0; the realized quantity is an
0.85-SD credibility loss in half the audience plus a measurable drop in COVID-information uptake.
**Grade:** A. **Direction:** the endorsement served the Democratic side; the *measured harm* falls on the endorsing
institution and on uptake by the other side. Both facts stand on the same paper.

## Item 45 — Data-access refusal

### 45-A. McKinsey diversity series vs Green & Hand 2024 — refusal is the finding — GROUNDED (A/B)
**Number as used:** McKinsey, *Why Diversity Matters* (2015), *Delivering Through Diversity* (2018), *Diversity Wins*
(2020), *Diversity Matters Even More* (2023) — "companies in the top quartile for racial/ethnic diversity are 35%
more likely to have financial returns above their national industry medians"; cited in corporate DEI programs,
investor-pressure campaigns, Nasdaq's board-diversity rule filings, and EEOC/state legislative debate.
**Primary record:** Jeremiah Green & Hand, "McKinsey's Diversity Matters/Delivers/Wins Results Revisited,"
*Econ Journal Watch* 21(1), March 2024, econjwatch.org/File+download/1296/GreenHandMar2024.pdf. Read 2026-09-16.
[LEAD CORRECTION] the brief's lead said *Econometrics Journal*; the paper is in **Econ Journal Watch**. Same authors,
same finding; venue in the lead was wrong.
**The refusal, verbatim:** "**McKinsey would not provide us with their detailed datasets, nor the names of the firms in
their datasets, so we were unable to directly replicate or investigate their analyses.**" Footnote 5 speculates the firms
are McKinsey clients under confidentiality.
**What the quasi-replication found (verbatim):** "we observe no statistically significant difference between the
likelihood of financial outperformance … **54.0 percent** of S&P 500 firms in the top executive race/ethnicity-ranked
quartile have a positive industry-adjusted EBIT margin vs. **51.2 percent** in the bottom quartile, with the z-statistic
on the difference of 2.8 percent being a **not statistically significant 0.5 (p-value = 0.65)**." For McKinsey's 5-category
metric the gap reverses: 51.3% vs 52.5%, z = −0.2. Null across six performance measures (EBIT margin, sales growth,
gross margin, ROA, ROE, TSR). McKinsey's own 2015 Exhibit 6: 61% vs 41%, z = 2.0, p = 0.05 (Green & Hand computed the
z; "McKinsey does not report a z-statistic in their exhibit 6").
**Construction defect beyond the null:** "the structure of McKinsey's tests are such that by measuring firm financial
performance over the four or five years **leading up to** the year in which they judge the race/ethnicity of firms'
executives, the default direction of causality … is that better firm financial performance causes firms to diversify …
not the reverse." The headline causal claim runs backwards in time.
**Grade:** A for the replication (published data/method); the McKinsey reports are **B** (published method, unpublished
firm list) and become effectively **D** on the causal claim since the sample cannot be inspected.
**Direction:** served DEI advocacy and corporate/regulatory diversity mandates.

## Item 42 — Political retraction / scientist-targeting

### 42-A. Johnson & Cesario, PNAS 2019 — retracted for MISUSE, with a real named error underneath — PARTIALLY CONTRADICTED (A)
**The claim as used:** PNAS significance statement, verbatim: "We find no evidence of anti-Black or anti-Hispanic
disparities across shootings, and **White officers are not more likely to shoot minority civilians than non-White
officers.**" Used by Heather Mac Donald (Manhattan Institute) in *City Journal* (2019) and the *Wall Street Journal*
(2020) to argue against racial bias in police shootings, and in congressional/police-reform debate post-Floyd.
**Primary record:** Cesario & Johnson, "Statement on the retraction of 'Officer characteristics and racial disparities
in fatal officer-involved shootings'," 6 July 2020, retractionwatch.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/PNAS_STATEMENT.pdf
(the authors' own PDF; PNAS page 403s to WebFetch). Read 2026-09-16.
**The stated reason, verbatim:** "We were careless when describing the inferences that could be made from our data.
This led to the **misuse of our article** to support the position that the probability of being shot by police did not
differ between Black and White Americans (MacDonald, 2019). To be clear, our work does not speak to this issue …
Although **our data and statistical approach were valid to estimate the question we actually tested** (the race of civilians
fatally shot by police), **given continued misuse of the article (e.g., MacDonald, 2020) we felt the right decision was to
retract the article** rather than publish further corrections."
**Why this is PARTIALLY CONTRADICTED as a "retracted for who cited it" case:** two things are true in the same document
and both must be reported. (a) The stated trigger for *retraction* (as opposed to the already-issued April 2020
correction) is continued citation by a named political writer, with the authors affirming their analysis was valid for
the question tested — that is the item-42 pattern. (b) There is also a genuine, named statistical error: the paper
estimated P(race | shot, X) while the significance statement asserted P(shot | race, X). Knox & Mummolo's PNAS comment
called the paper "mathematically incapable of supporting its central claims"; the authors credit them by name
("We are thankful to Knox and Mummolo (2020) for highlighting this error") and the April 2020 correction rewrote the
offending sentence to: "As the proportion of White officers in a fatal officer-involved shooting increased, a person
fatally shot was **not more likely** to be of a racial minority."
**And the authors pre-emptively denied the political reading.** They amended the statement to delete the Mac Donald
citations, and added, verbatim: "**This was the sole reason for our decision to retract the article; this decision had
nothing to do with political considerations, 'mob' pressure, threats to the authors, or distaste for the political views
of people citing the work approvingly.**" (Cesario to Retraction Watch, 8 July 2020: "people were incorrectly concluding
that we retracted due to either political pressure or the political views of those citing the paper. Neither is correct.")
**Grade:** A (authors' own signed statement, both versions available). **Direction:** the retracted result served the
right; the retraction served the left. The honest reading is that the *correction* was error-driven and the *escalation
from correction to retraction* was citation-driven by the authors' own account — a distinction the lead collapsed.

## Item 43 — Noble-lie communication

### 43-A. Fauci's herd-immunity threshold moved with polling, on record — GROUNDED (B)
**The number as used:** the share of the US population needing immunity for herd immunity — successively 60–70%,
then "70, 75%", then "75, 80, 85%" and "75 to 80-plus percent" (CNBC, 17 Dec 2020), used to set public expectations
for vaccination campaigns and reopening timelines.
**Primary record:** Donald G. McNeil Jr., "How much herd immunity is enough?", *New York Times*, 24 Dec 2020
(nytimes.com/2020/12/24/health/herd-immunity-covid-coronavirus.html — paywalled; read 2026-09-16 via the verbatim
NYT syndication at seattletimes.com/nation-world/how-much-herd-immunity-is-enough/ and capitalgazette.com,
both carrying the identical McNeil text).
**The on-record admission, verbatim:** "In a telephone interview the next day, Fauci acknowledged that **he had slowly but
deliberately been moving the goal posts.** He is doing so, he said, **partly based on new science, and partly on his gut
feeling that the country is finally ready to hear what he really thinks.**" And directly quoted: "**'When polls said only
about half of all Americans would take a vaccine, I was saying herd immunity would take 70 to 75%,' Fauci said. 'Then,
when newer surveys said 60% or more would take it, I thought, "I can nudge this up a bit," so I went to 80, 85.'**"
Plus the withheld upper bound: "'We really don't know what the real number is. I think the real range is somewhere
between 70 to 90%. **But, I'm not going to say 90%.**'" McNeil's gloss: saying 90% "might be discouraging to Americans."
**Construction:** the published point estimate is a function of two inputs — epidemiological parameters and a poll of
vaccine willingness. Only the first belongs in the estimand. The stated number was a *communication instrument*; the
speaker's own belief ("close to 90%") was withheld because of its anticipated behavioral effect.
**Alternative construction:** state the interval he actually held (70–90%) with its uncertainty, which he did state to
the reporter but not in the television interviews that set public expectation.
**Grade:** B (verbatim syndicated reprint of the primary interview; the NYT original is paywalled, so not graded A).
**Direction:** served the pro-vaccination public-health campaign, coded left in US 2020 politics. Fox News and The Hill
both ran it the same day, i.e. the admission was immediately usable by the other direction — and was.

### 40-C. Great Barrington Declaration vs John Snow Memorandum — consensus by signature, and the request to manufacture the takedown — GROUNDED (A)
**Pairing note (both-directions rule):** 40-A and 40-B are consensus claims that served the left; 40-C is a consensus
operation run against the right-coded side. Same evidence standard applied; both are in this section on purpose.
**The two documents:** the Great Barrington Declaration (4 Oct 2020; Bhattacharya, Kulldorff, Gupta; sponsored by the
American Institute for Economic Research) and the John Snow Memorandum (*The Lancet*, 14 Oct 2020) are both
*signature-count* instruments — neither reports a new estimate; each asserts a position and counts adherents. Both sides
used signature totals as evidence of consensus. Neither count is a sample of anything; self-selected signature lists have
no denominator, so no consensus proportion is computable from either. [INFERENCE] this is the structural defect shared
by both documents, symmetric.
**Primary record for the coordination:** US House of Representatives, Committee on Energy and Commerce, Subcommittee on
Communications and Technology, hearing "Preserving Free Speech and Reining in Big Tech Censorship," 28 March 2023,
Serial No. 118-15, govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-118hhrg54066/html/CHRG-118hhrg54066.htm — Jay Bhattacharya reading the
FOIA-obtained email into the record. Read 2026-09-16.
**Verbatim, as entered into the congressional record:** "Hi, Tony and Cliff. **This proposal from three fringe
epidemiologists** … who met with the Secretary seems to be getting a lot of attention. And even a cosignature from Nobel
Prize winner Mike Leavitt at Stanford. **There needs to be a quick and devastating published takedown of its premises.
I don't see anything like that online yet. Is it underway? Francis.**" (Francis Collins, then NIH Director, to Anthony
Fauci, 8 Oct 2020 — four days after the GBD was published.) Bhattacharya's characterization in the same transcript:
"It is possible to surmise from this email that Collins viewed the Great Barrington Declaration as a threat to the
illusion that there was a consensus."
**Construction:** the sequence is (1) a signature document appears, (2) the head of the funding agency requests that a
refutation be produced and published, (3) op-eds appear and are circulated between the two officials. The "takedown" is
commissioned *before* its content exists — the request specifies the conclusion and the desired effect ("devastating"),
not a question. [INFERENCE] the email is evidence of a requested outcome; it is not evidence that any specific published
paper was written in response.
**Grade:** A for the email text (read into a published congressional transcript; the underlying FOIA release is also
footnoted in a 17 Dec 2021 House staff report). The *interpretation* that this suppressed the GBD is C — not established
by this document. **Direction:** the operation ran against the right-coded/anti-lockdown side.

## Item 44 — Evidence-hierarchy asymmetry

### 44-A. Cochrane masks review 2023 — "inconclusive" read as "masks don't work" and as "masks work" — GROUNDED (A), with a contradiction of the standard telling
**The claim as used:** Jefferson et al., "Physical interventions to interrupt or reduce the spread of respiratory
viruses," Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 30 Jan 2023, doi 10.1002/14651858.CD006207.pub6. Used by
anti-mandate commentators and legislators as proof that "masks don't work"; used by mandate defenders, after the
editor's statement, as proof that the review said nothing against masks.
**Primary record:** Cochrane, "Statement on 'Physical interventions to interrupt or reduce the spread of respiratory
viruses' review," 10 March 2023, cochrane.org/about-us/news/statement-physical-interventions-interrupt-or-reduce-spread-
respiratory-viruses-review. Read 2026-09-16 (the page is JS-rendered; curl and WebFetch return chrome only —
recovered via Exa's cached full text, cross-checked verbatim against FactCheck.org, AFP and Forbes reproductions).
**Verbatim (Karla Soares-Weiser, Editor-in-Chief):** "**Many commentators have claimed that a recently-updated Cochrane
Review shows that 'masks don't work', which is an inaccurate and misleading interpretation. It would be accurate to say
that the review examined whether interventions to promote mask wearing help to slow the spread of respiratory viruses,
and that the results were inconclusive. Given the limitations in the primary evidence, the review is not able to address
the question of whether mask-wearing itself reduces people's risk of contracting or spreading respiratory viruses.**"
Quoting the review's own abstract: "The high risk of bias in the trials, variation in outcome measurement, and relatively
low adherence with the interventions during the studies hampers drawing firm conclusions." And the adherence figure that
makes the estimand a policy estimand: "**in the most heavily-weighted trial of interventions to promote community mask
wearing, 42.3% of people in the intervention arm wore masks compared to 13.3% of those in the control arm.**"
**The asymmetry, stated precisely:** the trials randomize *encouragement to mask*, with a 29-point compliance contrast,
so the estimand is the intention-to-treat effect of a mask *promotion policy*, not of a mask. Reading it as "masks don't
work" substitutes the per-protocol effect of the device. Reading the editor's statement as "the review supports masks"
substitutes an absence of evidence for evidence of effect in the other direction. **The same "inconclusive" was read as
"no effect" by one side and as "no finding against" by the other.** Both readings are unsupported by the same document.
**CONTRADICTION of the standard telling:** the March 2023 statement promised an updated plain language summary. The same
Cochrane page now carries, verbatim: "**June 2024 update: Following engagement with the authors, the decision has been
made that changes to the plain language summary and abstract would not impact the scientific integrity of the content
and so Cochrane is no longer seeking updates to the plain language summary and abstract of this version of the review.**"
The disputed original wording — "We are uncertain whether wearing masks or N95/P2 respirators helps to slow the spread
of respiratory viruses based on the studies we assessed" — therefore still stands. Accounts on both sides that stop at
the March 2023 apology ("for which we apologize … we take responsibility for not making the wording clearer") are
15 months out of date.
**Grade:** A (publisher's own statement, plus the review's own abstract quoted inside it).
**Direction:** the review's headline served the anti-mandate side; the editor's statement served the pro-mandate side;
the 2024 retraction-of-the-correction served neither and was reported by almost no one.

## Disconfirmation record

1. **Lead 5 (PNAS) is partially contradicted by its own primary.** The authors explicitly wrote: "This was the sole
   reason for our decision to retract the article; this decision had nothing to do with political considerations, 'mob'
   pressure, threats to the authors, or distaste for the political views of people citing the work approvingly," and
   deleted the Mac Donald citations from the amended statement. A real, named statistical error (P(race|shot) vs

   supports and their second-version text disputes. Both versions are quoted above.
2. **Lead 12 venue was wrong.** Green & Hand is in *Econ Journal Watch* 21(1), March 2024 — not the *Econometrics

3. **Lead 11 was incomplete.** The Cochrane plain-language-summary correction promised in March 2023 was abandoned in
   June 2024; the original wording stands. Any memo citing the March statement as the end of the story is stale.
4. **Cook et al. is not itself the misuse.** The paper publishes 66.4% no-position in its own abstract. The defect is
   in the political rendering ("97% of scientists"), not in the paper's reporting of its denominator. A lane that
   graded the paper rather than the use would get this backwards.
