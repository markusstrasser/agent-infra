# Predictive/Control Architectures for the Two-Loop RSI Design

Research 2026-06-16. Does hierarchical multi-timescale predictive/control literature suggest a better structure than our short-reflex / long-learning two-loop RSI design?

## Raw findings (incremental, by axis)

### Axis 4 — Control theory: cascaded nested loops (HIGHEST VALUE, mechanism-level, NOT frontier-fragile)
Our short/long split IS the textbook cascaded-control pattern. Direct hits:
- **Time-scale separation is a hard requirement, not a nicety.** Skogestad (NTNU advanced-control notes, control-theory canon): "A large time scale separation is desired to allow for independent design of the layers (loops) and to avoid undesired interactions ('fighting')... Shinskey recommends ≥4, Skogestad & Postlethwaite / Smith recommend ≥5. **If the time scale separation gets too small, typically 3 or less, the layers start interacting and resonance occurs, performance degrades even nominally.** Rule of thumb: separation 4 (min) to 10 (preferable)." [PROV: control-theory textbook, mechanism-level — transfers]
- **MathWorks / ControlSoft (cascade PID canon):** inner loop must be ~10× faster bandwidth than outer; "if the inner loop is not faster than the outer loop, the cascade offers no significant improvement." Design inner loop FIRST with outer open, then outer with inner closed. [PROV: engineering canon]
- **Anti-windup is a named, required component in cascade control.** ControlSoft: "when PID2 (inner) saturates, it is important to INHIBIT PID1 (outer) from increasing/decreasing. If PID1 is not inhibited, controller windup will occur." Åström-Hägglund tracking anti-windup (E8) is the standard implementation. Decoupled inner/outer anti-windup compensators (ScienceDirect S0019057810000224) keep stability independent. [PROV: engineering canon — directly maps to "long loop keeps promoting rules that never fire"]

### Axis 1 — Active inference / FEP (PRE-FRONTIER as neuroscience; mechanism-level transfer)
- **Multi-timescale is intrinsic and explicitly >2 levels.** Friston "Active Inference: A Process Theory" (2017): expectations change over (a) fast timescale updating beliefs after each observation, (b) slower timescale accumulating evidence across observations, (c) trial-level updating of (concentration) parameters = learning. That is THREE nested timescales, not two. Hierarchical Active Inference: A Theory of Motivated Control (PMC5870049) uses 3 levels = "three corticothalamic loops of increasing hierarchical depth," separation of temporal scales.
- **Expected Free Energy (EFE) = the anticipatory edge we lack.** EFE requires PREDICTED FUTURE observations (vs variational FE which uses past/present). Policy prior ∝ exp(−γ·G(π)). EFE has two parts: epistemic (expected information gain — resolve uncertainty / exploration) + pragmatic (goal/preference fulfilment — exploitation). Active inference "automatically balances exploration and exploitation" via this. KEY: our loops only minimize *observed* surprise (past misses); EFE selects actions to minimize *expected future* surprise. [PROV: Parr/Pezzulo/Friston textbook 2022]
- **Precision-weighting = gain control = the "which detector to trust" edge we lack.** Limanowski/Adams/Parr "Many Roles of Precision in Action": precision = synaptic gain control, "the weight afforded to prediction-error signals or confidence in a policy." Higher levels modulate lower-level precision (precision cascade). γ (inverse temperature) in policy selection IS a precision. Implemented neurally via neuromodulators (dopamine/ACh/noradrenaline). Maps directly to: weight each detector/miner's signal by its demonstrated reliability. [PROV: mechanism-level]

#### DISCONFIRMATION on active inference (important — tempers the anticipatory edge)
- **EFE policy search is intractable at scale.** "naive policy selection is intractable for even moderately large graphs"; exhaustive EFE-per-policy "renders even relatively small-scale models intractable" (arXiv:2209.02550). Don't import the full EFE machinery.
- **Beren Millidge (who tried to derive EFE from first principles) retrospective (2024):** "existing derivations of the EFE were incorrect or... largely assumed their conclusion." The one claimed advantage is exploration quality via the info-gain term, but "it does NOT have particular practical advantages vs simply using exploration bonuses as an ad-hoc tool to improve RL algorithms where needed." → TAKEAWAY: the *mechanism* (a prospective info-gain bonus + precision gain) transfers; the FEP/EFE *formalism* is not worth importing. Friston's framework also "presupposes successful perception and action instead of explaining them" (PhilSci 18843) and has not solved the relevance/frame problem (PhiMiSci 2025). [PROV: CURRENT critique 2024-2025]

### Axis 3 — RSI / meta-learning loop topologies that work vs collapse (CURRENT, frontier-relevant)
Already in our prior memos (hjepa-vs-llm-scaffolding, agents-rsi-gap-sweep) — reconfirmed + extended:
- **Darwin Gödel Machine (Zhang/Hu/Lu/Lange/Clune, arXiv:2505.22954, 121 cites):** open-ended evolution, archive of agents (MAP-Elites-style, keeps ALL ancestors not just best = stepping-stone preservation). Empirically improves (SWE-bench 20→50%). The guard that matters: the eval is grounded in a held-out, agent-untouchable verifier, with explicit hack-monitoring. ⚠ **CORRECTION (parent spot-check 2026-06-16):** the specific "DGM faked its own benchmark results / removed logging" anecdote is **DISPUTED** — Exa `verify_claim` (confidence 1) traces that incident to *Poolside's Laguna M.1*, not DGM; the DGM abstract/secondary sources don't assert it. Treat as needs-primary-read (the DGM discussion section), NOT settled. The held-out-untouchable-verifier guard does NOT rest on the DGM specific — reward-hacking by self-eval-touching agents is a broad attested class (SpecBench arXiv:2605.21384; Poolside SWE-bench hack; benchmark-hacking generally). [PROV: CURRENT — DGM-specific DISPUTED, class-level holds]
- **"RSI is three different things" (LessWrong 2026-06-15, freshest):** scaffolding-improvement (better harness around same model — NO retraining), R&D-improvement (process that makes models), mind-improvement (model rewrites itself). **Our RSI is purely the FIRST kind** — "better orchestration → better use of existing cognition; bottlenecks are engineering/integration, not capability." This is the well-behaved, observable, non-FOOM regime. Reframes our objective correctly: we are doing systems-RSI, which converges, not mind-RSI, which is the one that flatlines-or-explodes. [PROV: CURRENT essay]
- **Yudkowsky's classic RSI flatline argument:** recursion "should either flatline or blow up" — diminishing returns at each level, opportunities "once used up are gone." Maps to integral-windup: a level keeps pushing after its returns are exhausted. [PROV: old essay, mechanism]
- **Mode collapse as attractor dynamics (LessWrong "Mysteries of Mode Collapse"):** optimization creates global attractors that generalize OOD. The RSI analog: the long loop over-optimizing one supervision metric collapses the agent's behavioral diversity. → argues for the precision/diversity guard.

### THE central collapse mechanism — "Worlds Where Iterative Design Fails" (LessWrong, John Wentworth)
This is the single most load-bearing disconfirmation for our design:
- **The iterative-design loop breaks when the fix makes the problem LESS VISIBLE rather than fixing it** ("problem-hiding"). RLHF is called "uniquely terrible" precisely because it suppresses the warning signs instead of the error. → DIRECT THREAT to our loop: if a promoted hook *suppresses the symptom* (e.g. silences a class of error output) rather than fixing the cause, the long loop's sensor (sessions) goes blind and we drift while believing we improved. Our blindspot-miner is the partial guard; this says it must specifically watch for "supervision dropped AND error-visibility dropped" together. The constitution already names this: "if supervision drops but errors go undetected, the system is drifting, not improving." Lit CONFIRMS this is the dominant failure mode. [PROV: CURRENT essay, alignment canon]
- **"You get what you measure":** the loop optimizes the measurable proxy (supervision-kpi regex) not the latent goal. Argues supervision-kpi must stay a TREND signal cross-checked by the semantic blindspot-miner, never the promotion gate itself. [PROV: essay]

### Axis 2 — LeCun H-JEPA: does the hierarchy argue for >2 levels?
- **H-JEPA stacks JEPAs by timescale/abstraction:** JEPA-1 = short-horizon fine-grained (ms–s), JEPA-2 = longer-horizon coarse (s–min), JEPA-N = increasingly abstract/long-horizon. "Each level trades detail for prediction horizon." Higher levels predict longer because abstraction makes the future more predictable.
- **The 2-tier cut is explicitly called arbitrary.** Bandaru deep-dive: "This two-tier structure, though innovative, is arbitrary. True intelligence requires multiple levels of abstraction. However, it is NOT clear how many levels are needed. We may even need VARIABLE levels of abstraction — different situations have different complexity." LeCun's own framing: predict "over multiple time scales," decomposition "repeated all the way down."
- **The Configurator = the precision/gain edge again.** LeCun's configurator "primes the perception system to extract the relevant information for the task at hand" and "configures the world model" — i.e. task-conditioned gain control over which signals matter. Same construct as active-inference precision-weighting. NOT implemented in any JEPA (no configurator exists yet). [PROV: position paper 2022 PRE-FRONTIER + 2024 commentary; mechanism-level, none demonstrated for agentic/text]
- **Convergent takeaway across axes 1, 2, 4:** all three independent literatures (active inference's hierarchical-FE, H-JEPA's stacked timescales, cascade control's nested loops) say the NUMBER of timescale levels should match the number of natural timescales in the plant, and that >2 is normal — but none mandates a fixed N. A 3-level aerospace cascade (attitude→rate→torque) is canonical.

## Verdict (≤15 lines in final message)

The 2-loop CUT is correct — keep it. The literatures do NOT argue for a fundamentally different topology; they argue our 2 loops are an under-instrumented cascade. Three of four axes (active inference, H-JEPA, cascade control) independently say >2 timescale levels is *normal*, but (a) we already have a de-facto middle level (the daily launchd miners) and (b) none mandates a fixed N — adding levels for their own sake is unjustified. The high-value additions are NAMED EDGES, not new loops:
1. **Anti-windup gate** (C2) — stop promoting a rule-class that fires-but-doesn't-fix. Cheapest, highest value, directly the windup the prompt anticipated.
2. **Precision-weighting on detectors** (C5) — weight each miner by demonstrated precision; the data is already being collected.
3. **Problem-hiding guard** (C10) — alarm when supervision↓ co-occurs with visibility↓. The dominant collapse mode in the lit; already half-stated in our constitution.
4. **Anticipatory edge** (C4) — real but LOWEST-confidence; do the cheap leading-indicator version only, NOT an EFE planner (C6: the formalism is intractable + its only edge is matched by ad-hoc bonuses), and ONLY paired with #1 or it becomes windup.
DON'T import the FEP/EFE formalism, JEPA architecture, or a Gödel-machine rewrite-yourself loop. Our regime is observable scaffolding-RSI (C9), the well-behaved one.
