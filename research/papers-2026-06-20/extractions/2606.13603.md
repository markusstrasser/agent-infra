**Mechanism**

1. Generate a full chain-of-thought `C` for prompt `P`.
2. Split `C` into sentence-level spans `C1...Cn` using terminal punctuation.
3. Build truncated prefixes:
   `Xi = P + [BOT] + Ci-prefix + [EOT] + S`, where `S` forces a direct final answer.
4. For each prefix `Xi`, greedily decode an answer `Âi`.
5. Compare `Âi` to the full-CoT answer `Ân`, not to ground truth. This measures when the model stabilizes to its own final answer, not when it becomes correct.
6. Compute step confidence `pi` as the probability of the answer token under the prefix condition. Discard traces with first-token collisions where different semantic answers share the same first token.
7. Label each step:
   `NO-GUESS`: `pi <= τ`
   `MID-GUESS`: `pi > τ` and `Âi != Ân`
   `FINAL-GUESS`: `pi > τ` and `Âi == Ân`
8. Define the commitment boundary `i* = argmax_i(pi - pi-1)`: the sentence causing the largest confidence jump toward the final answer.
9. Stress-test causality by corrupting numeric literals before vs. after `i*`; post-boundary corruption should matter less if the tail is epiphenomenal.
10. Train lightweight causal attention probes on model hidden states at sentence boundaries to predict `NO/MID/FINAL-GUESS`.
11. Use probe firing on consecutive `FINAL-GUESS` predictions as an online early-exit trigger; truncate the remaining CoT and force the answer.

**Why it works**

The causal claim is that answer formation in reasoning traces is often discontinuous: one sentence sharply moves the model from no/mid-guess to its final answer distribution. After this “commitment boundary,” later CoT tokens may look deliberative but no longer causally affect the forced final answer.

The theoretical bet is twofold: first, truncation is cleaner than perturbing reasoning text because it avoids asking the model to interpret edited, unnatural CoT. Second, if commitment is a stable internal mechanism rather than a surface artifact, hidden states around sentence boundaries should linearly encode the answer-formation stage.

Skeptical note: this is commitment to the model’s own full-CoT answer under an answer-forcing suffix, not commitment to truth, calibrated confidence, or behavior in later interactive turns.

**Preconditions**

The method requires:

- Access to model logprobs and hidden states for probe training; this rules out most closed frontier APIs.
- A task with extractable/verifiable final answers.
- Model-specific beginning/end-of-thinking markers.
- Reasoning traces that can be segmented into meaningful sentence-level steps.
- A suffix `S` that can force a direct answer without too badly distorting behavior.
- A semantic equivalence verifier for candidate answers.
- Filtering of cases where no-CoT already answers correctly, because they study traces where CoT improves output.
- Filtering of first-token collisions between semantically different answers.
- A reasonably stable “single final answer” notion; agentic/tool-use tasks may not satisfy this.

**Measured results**

Models: `gpt-oss-20b` OpenAI 2025, `Qwen3-14B` 2025, `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it` Google 2026. These are open-weight/non-frontier relative to current closed frontier systems; the paper explicitly does not study closed-weight frontier models.

Tasks: MATH-500, AIME 2025, ZebraLogic, GPQA-Diamond.

Generation: vLLM, `max_model_len=16,384`, `temperature=0.7`, `top_p=0.9`; reasoning effort medium where available. `gpt-oss-20b`: 16 traces/question. Other models: 8 traces/question, attribution on up to 4 valid traces/question.

- `gpt-oss-20b`, AIME 2025, `n=158`, three samples/setting.

- At 50% corruption: PRE 27%; POST 72%.

- `gpt-oss-20b` MATH-500 mid-guesses average likelihood `0.69`.

- Across independent traces of the same MATH-500 question, `gpt-oss-20b` median mid-guess Jaccard `0.71`; median relative-position SD `0.005` vs random baseline `0.011`.

Early exit:
- On MATH-500, fixed-percentage truncation lost 23 points full-CoT accuracy: 80% to 57%.
- Probe at `k=1`: 71% vs 80% full-CoT accuracy, saving 39% CoT tokens.
- Probe at `k=5`: 78% vs 80%, saving 26%.
- OOD application to AIME 2025, ZebraLogic, GPQA-Diamond: maximum reported loss 11 points on ZebraLogic, 97% to 86%; token savings 25%, 6%, and 35% respectively.
- Some claims in figures lack extractable CIs/N from the text; do not treat the smooth curves as precisely quantified beyond the reported numbers.

**Limitations / failure modes / non-transfer**

- Greedy decoding only sees argmax answers; distributional shifts below argmax are invisible.
- The answer-forcing suffix is an intervention. It may force commitment earlier or differently than natural continuation.
- The method measures commitment to the model’s own full-CoT answer, not correctness.
- Sentence-level segmentation is coarse; true boundary may happen mid-sentence or token-level.
- Filters bias the sample toward cases where CoT helps and where answers are token-distinguishable.
- Probe labels inherit truncation-method biases.
- Activation probes require internals; not usable for closed frontier APIs.
- Tested domains are math, logic, and multiple-choice science, not code agents, tool-using agents, long-horizon workflows, or open-ended synthesis.
- Post-boundary tokens may still matter for confidence calibration, follow-up turns, RL reward shaping, or alternate decoding, even if they do not change the forced final answer.
- Early exit has nonzero risk: OOD early-fire up to 21% for `Qwen3-14B`.
- Surface hedging words are not reliable indicators; words like “wait,” “but,” and “check” appear both before and after commitment.

Concrete output: proposals to shorten decision tails when the commitment boundary is empirically early.

Bolt-on: a Stop-hook advisory for long answers that contain repeated hedging/rechecking after a concrete conclusion is already stated. `MEMORY`: Applicable. Bolt-on: store links from condensed lessons to raw session spans marking “commitment point” and “post-commitment correction/tail.” This directly addresses the primer’s gap: condensed-to-raw links are not first-class.

`/eval`: Strongly applicable. `predict-then-falsify`: Strongly applicable. Bolt-on: before a harness edit intended to reduce over-asking or verbose post-commitment reasoning, pre-register: “post-commitment tail tokens/session decrease without increasing correction rate.” Accept only if session-trace shows the intended mechanism fired.

`Workflow`: Applicable. Bolt-on: add a workflow phase marker for “commitment point” in file-bus runs: once the orchestrator has selected an action and named the verifier, later steps must either execute/probe or explicitly reopen the decision with new evidence. This is a structural version of the paper’s boundary idea, not model-internal probing.

`ToolSearch`: Limited applicability. Bolt-on: use ToolSearch as the lazy-load analogue of early exit: once the needed tool family is identified, stop expanding tool surface unless a verifier fails. This is not the paper’s mechanism, but it transfers the “avoid post-commitment continuation” pattern to tool discovery.

`NOT-APPLICABLE`: activation-probe early exit for live Claude/Codex/Cursor sessions. Reason: our system is a single-operator local harness over subscription/CLI tools, not an open-weight runtime with hidden-state access. We should pattern-extract the truncation/commitment-boundary eval, not adopt probe infrastructure or a hosted runtime.
